01 – The Memory Book

The Name

Vietnamese–EnglishN

no nê

adj. /naw neh/

Fully satisfied after a good meal: when your stomach is full and your heart is happy.

“No Nê” is a Vietnamese expression that describes more than just being full. It's the feeling of satisfaction, comfort, and joy after a good meal — when your stomach is full and your heart is happy.

At No Nê Kitchen & Bar, we believe food should do more than fill you up. We serve comforting Vietnamese classics, made to be enjoyed slowly and remembered fondly.

  • Full belly No bụng
  • Light heart Nhẹ lòng
  • Happy Vui vẻ
  • Remembering the taste Nhớ mãi hương vị

Come hungry. Eat till you're full. Leave happy.

02 – The Memory Book

A Time To Remember

For Vietnamese people, the subsidy era was not just a time of hardship. It is an unforgettable part of memory.

Sổ Tem Phiếu · Ration Book

Tear a coupon to read a memory

It was the sight of long queues, ration books held tightly in hand.

Goods were scarce, and essentials like rice, sugar and fabric were distributed in fixed portions.

People waited patiently, learned to save, and cherished even the smallest things they received.

Memories found: 0 / 6

Meals were simple, yet always filled with warmth and shared laughter.

To remember the clinking of bowls and chopsticks in a small home.

To remember the loving eyes of grandparents and parents watching their children eat with quiet joy.

Back then, every gram of food mattered.

Ngày ấy, từng gram đều quý.

No Nê Kitchen & Bar was not created to chase modern glamour, but to preserve those memories. Memories of the subsidy era, when life was simple, yet human connection ran deep.

Archival photographs of the bao cấp era.

04 – The Memory Book

Find the Details

Every corner holds a memory. Tap the dots to explore the dining room.

  1. Street sign A blue enamel street sign. Hanoi's Old Quarter streets are named after the goods once sold there, like Hàng Nón (conical hats).
  2. Striped pole A red and white striped pole, like the utility poles on old Hanoi streets.
  3. Tin awning A corrugated tin awning, a familiar sight on small Hanoi shopfronts.
  4. Blue shutters Blue wooden window shutters, a classic detail of old Hanoi houses.
  5. Hanging garlic and snacks Garlic and snacks hanging by the counter, just like in a neighbourhood grocery.
  6. Thermos A vintage flower-patterned thermos, found in almost every Vietnamese home.
  7. Floral cushion Red floral fabric, the bold pattern found on blankets and cushions in many Vietnamese homes.
